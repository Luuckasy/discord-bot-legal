Privacy Policy

Index-Tan · Last updated 2026-11-23

This policy explains what data the Index-Tan Discord application ("the bot") processes, why, and how long it is kept.

1. Data we process

  • Roblox game data: Roblox user ID, username and display name, join/leave times and session length, server identifiers, and the text, category and technical details of bug reports sent from inside our games.
  • Discord data, only in the channels the server staff set up as bug-report or suggestion channels: the text of messages posted there and their attachments (images, videos, files), the author's Discord user ID, username and display name, the reaction counts on those messages, the beginning of the message a post replies to (for context), and message, channel and server IDs.
  • Community channels, only in the servers of our games that the developer has set up: the text of messages posted in the channels every regular member can see (never staff-only channels, tickets or private threads), the images attached to them, which message a message replies to, the channel name, and whether the author has a staff role. Usernames and user IDs are not kept: the bot only counts how many different people talked in each hour.
  • Rules and moderation channels, only the ones the developer lists for a server: the rules channels, punishment logs written by the staff or by moderation bots, AutoMod and anti-scam alerts, punishment appeals, and the staff's moderation commands and discussion. Usernames, mentions and IDs are removed automatically before this text is processed. Member join, leave and name-change logs are never read.
  • Replies the bot sends on behalf of the developers in the bug-report and suggestion channels.

Apart from the moderation channels listed by the developer, the bot does not read staff-only channels, tickets, private threads or direct messages. We do not collect passwords, payment information, e-mail addresses, server member lists or presence/activity status.

2. How we use it

  • To acknowledge each report with a reaction (📚 bug, 💡 suggestion).
  • To classify messages as bug, suggestion or general chat, group duplicate reports and keep the development team's internal task list, and to mark a report with ✅ when it is fixed and 🚀 when the fix is released.
  • To reply to a report through the bot, for example to ask for a video, the steps to reproduce or the device used, or to explain that something is not a bug. Replies are sent by the development team, sometimes written with the help of AI assistants. In servers where the developer turned it on, the bot can also reply automatically to a bug report that is missing details, with a fixed message asking for a video or screenshot, the steps, or the device; it does this at most once per report.
  • To write, once per hour, an anonymous summary for the developers of how the community feels about the game (a mood score, common complaints, praise, bugs, requests and open questions), so they can understand players and prioritize fixes.
  • To keep a knowledge base about each game made only from answers and statements written by the server staff and from the official update log (for example "how do I unlock X?" and the staff's answer). Messages from regular players are never stored as facts. The bot does not reply to anyone in community channels.
  • When the community reading is set up for a server, the previous 30 days of the same channels may be read once, in the same way and with the same limits, so the summaries and the knowledge base start with some history.
  • To help the staff moderate: to learn the server rules, to summarize how the staff applies them (which kind of infraction received which action, without names), and to point out, for the staff to review, messages in community channels that may break a rule or show that someone may be at risk (for example talk of self-harm). These are only suggestions: the bot never punishes, deletes messages or contacts anyone, and every decision is made by a person on the staff.
  • To relay bug reports and player activity from our Roblox games to the development team's Discord channels.

We do not sell data, show ads, build profiles, or use Discord data to train or fine-tune AI models.

3. Automated classification

The AI processing described below is done by Anthropic (Claude models) and, as a backup when it is unavailable, by Groq (the "AI services"). Neither receives Discord usernames, display names or user IDs.

To classify and group reports, the text of messages from those channels (and the text they reply to), the channel name and the reaction counts are sent to the AI services. The result decides how a report is listed in the developers' task list and whether the bot asks for missing details.

For the hourly community summary, the text of the messages of that hour from the community channels (mentions removed, links reduced to the site name) and up to six attached images per hour are sent to the AI services. Each image is only described in one sentence (for example "game screen showing a connection error"); people in photos are not identified, and the image is not saved. From time to time a second model reviews a sample of this work against the same messages, to check its accuracy.

For moderation, the text of the rules channels and of the listed moderation channels is sent to the AI services after usernames, mentions and IDs are removed. A name written inside free text may remain in what is sent; it is not kept in the results.

4. Storage and retention

  • The bot service runs on Railway. Grouping of duplicate in-game reports is kept in memory for up to 24 hours and erased whenever the service restarts.
  • Messages from the bug-report and suggestion channels (and copies of attachments saved to investigate a report) are kept in a private archive on the developer's computer for up to 90 days and then deleted automatically.
  • The internal task list keeps a short summary written by the team and a link to the original message, as part of our development records. It does not copy the message text or user IDs.
  • Messages from community channels are kept only in the bot's memory for up to 2 hours and 10 minutes, to write the hourly summary, and are never written to disk; a message deleted in Discord is dropped from that memory. Images are held in memory only while they are described.
  • Hourly community summaries (no usernames or IDs; links to the staff messages they cite) are kept for up to 90 days. After that only a short record of each hour is kept, to chart how the community mood changes over time: the mood score, the number of messages, people and staff messages, and the short anonymous summary text, with no links, usernames or IDs.
  • The knowledge base keeps short rewritten questions and the staff's answers, with a link to the staff message, while the information is still useful for the game. It contains no usernames or IDs.
  • Messages from moderation channels are kept only in the bot's memory for up to 2 hours and 10 minutes. Messages pointed out for staff review are kept as a short description with a link to the message for up to 90 days. The learned rules, the anonymous summary of how the staff punishes (infraction type, action, duration), staff decisions and the bot's own observations, and the anonymous topics of what the community asks, likes and complains about are kept while useful; they contain no usernames or IDs.
  • The developers see these summaries in a private web dashboard served by the bot on Railway and protected by a password. The copy of the summaries it shows is kept only in the service's memory.
  • Messages remain in Discord under Discord's own Privacy Policy until they are deleted by their author or the server staff.

5. Sharing

Data is shared only with Discord (to read and post messages), our hosting provider (Railway) to run the service, and the AI services (Anthropic, and Groq as a backup) for the automated processing described above. We do not share it with anyone else, except when required by law.

6. Your choices

You can avoid having your Discord messages processed by not posting in the bug-report and suggestion channels or in the public channels of a server where the bot reads the community. To have a message removed from our archive, task list, summaries or knowledge base, contact the developer through the Discord server where the bot is installed with the message link; we delete it within 30 days. Server admins can exclude any channel from the community reading, or remove the bot, at any time.

7. Children

Our games and this bot follow the age requirements of Roblox and Discord. We do not knowingly collect more data from children than described above.

8. Changes

We may update this policy; the date at the top shows the latest version.
